# Results summary (Stage C1; development data and simulation only)

**1. Theory holds.** Independent derivation and simulation confirm Prop. 1 (χ² laws, independence, E[Q], E[R], c). In the 90 Gaussian cells of
`gaussian_t_validation.csv`, all 180 z-statistics for E[Q] and E[R] are within |z| ≤ 3 (max 2.70; 4 of 180 exceed 2, expected ≈ 8 by chance).
The corrected forecast's level bias against the true variance is at most 1.53% in magnitude in every Gaussian cell (max |z| 2.34).

**2. c is level-unbiased but not ratio-unbiased (new, M1).** Under Gaussian data the mean of (true variance)/(corrected forecast) is
(T−N)/(T−N−2) > 1 (1.069 at N=30, T=60, averaged over three covariance structures) while its median is 1.003. The QLIKE-optimal constant is
c_qlike = c·(T−N)/(T−N−2). On a 60-day block target c_qlike lowers QLIKE slightly even under Gaussian data (N=30, T=60: 0.071 → 0.069).

**3. Heavy tails leave a large residual miscalibration with a known covariance.** Mean level bias of the c-corrected forecast
(known-Σ target, all N,T,structures): Gaussian −0.0%, t15 −4.8%, t8 −10.0%, t5 −18.6% (worst cell −38.6%). At N=30, T=60: t15 −10.2%,
t8 −19.2%, t5 −32.6% (the raw forecast is biased by about −34% to −43% on average). So Gaussian calibration is incomplete when tails are thick; t5 is an extreme setting.
In heavy-tailed cells c_qlike has lower mean absolute log error than c (t5: 0.244 vs 0.264) but still under-forecasts.

**4. Development-sample baseline reproduces the conference numbers.** For H = 20 the re-implementation matches Table II's raw, corrected and LW QLIKE and variance
ratios exactly (see CLAIM_TRACEABILITY C08). Paired tests (corrected vs raw/clipped/MP/LW, 24 per horizon, block length 3 for 24 periods, Holm within horizon):
mean-difference interval excludes 0 in 10 (H=20) and 8 (H=60); both intervals favour the correction in 2 (H=20) and 5 (H=60); Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 in 2 and 1.
This is weak, as the conference paper already said for the 24-period sample. Dropping the 6 vendor-holiday rows (P3) leaves this essentially unchanged (8/3/2 and 8/5/1).

**5. Conference Table II and abstract counts are reproduced (new, Stage C1b).** With NL and BPS re-implemented and the 2011-2026 data re-downloaded,
all 14 Table II rows reproduce: raw, corrected, LW and NL QLIKE to 3 decimals; variance ratios within rounding; paired differences, intervals and win rates within bootstrap noise
(interval ends within about 0.02, win-rate ends within about 4 points). Counts: corrected has lower mean QLIKE in 348/348 comparisons (paper 347/348; the one paper exception involves BPS, which I re-implemented as a
finite-sample analogue, not verbatim); mean-difference CI below zero in 220/276 extended comparisons (paper 219); all 132 at W<=120 with both-interval wins in 125 (paper 126);
11/48 at W=250 (exact); 0/96 at W>=500 (exact); 5 significant losses, all universe A 2011-26 at W=750 (exact); 8/72 in development (exact). BPS matches the paper at W>=250 and differs by up to 0.03 QLIKE at W<=90.

**6. Temporal dependence, not only tail shape, breaks conditional calibration (Stage C2).** With the unconditional covariance fixed at Sigma and 3000 replications per cell
(N in {10, 30}, T in {60, 120, 250}): mean level bias of the c-corrected forecast against the unconditional true variance is +0.1% (i.i.d.), -2.6% (GARCH, worst cell -5.0%) and -6.3% (regime switching, worst -12.5%),
compared with -4.8% / -10.0% / -18.6% for Student-t df 15 / 8 / 5. On the 60-day block, realised/forecast (ratio of means) is 1.00 (i.i.d.), 1.03 (GARCH), 1.07 (regime), but conditional on the origin it varies much more:
regime process 1.24 when the origin is in the stress state vs 1.03 in calm; 1.28 if the state switches inside the test block vs 0.81 if it does not; GARCH 1.19 after high recent volatility vs 0.88 after low. c_qlike has lower block QLIKE than c in every process
(i.i.d. 0.036 -> 0.035, GARCH 0.136 -> 0.132, regime 0.144 -> 0.139).

**7. The (N,T) calibration surface reproduces (Stage C3).** Measured/Gaussian ratio exceeds 1 in all 60 cells (ranges 1.003-1.244 and 1.008-1.169; paper 1.011-1.244 and 1.006-1.167); fits 1.038 + 0.463 N/T (r 0.85) and 1.063 + 0.169 N/T (r 0.78) (paper 1.039 + 0.473, 0.86 and 1.060 + 0.179, 0.80;
the exact subset draws are not specified in the paper). New: with origin-block bootstrap intervals that keep the subset dimension, the interval excludes 1 in 0 of 30 development cells (only 8 origins) and 14 of 30 cells of the 2011-26 sample; the slope interval is [-0.006, 1.36] (dev) and [0.065, 0.287] (2011-26).
So "exceeds the Gaussian theory in all 60 cells" is a statement about point estimates; the dependence-aware support is much weaker, especially in development.

**8. Regime-conditional performance (Stage C3).** Origins are labelled by terciles of trailing 60-day equal-weight volatility, with thresholds from rows before 750 only. In 2011-26 universe A at W=60, H=20: realised/forecast (corrected) is 1.46 after low-vol origins (median ratio 1.29),
1.15 after high-vol origins (median 0.78, so the forecast is usually too high), and 1.51 for mid. The paired QLIKE difference corrected minus NL is -0.455 [-0.607, -0.306] (low), -1.282 [-3.505, -0.146] (mid) and +0.086 [-0.076, 0.363] (high): the advantage over NL is not shown in the high-volatility regime.
The six origins whose test block overlaps the Feb-Jun 2020 window are too few for inference (corrected QLIKE 4.64, NL 14.05 vs 0.21 and 0.50 elsewhere). All development-sample regime cells have n = 6-10 and are flagged inadequate.

**9. Second-stage calibration is a negative result (Stage C4).** Models: constant multiplier (QLIKE-optimal mean of past v/h_G), causal-volatility-tercile multipliers, and a ridge log-linear model on five causal features
(log recent/whole-window variance, PC1 share, average correlation, excess kurtosis, last matured calibration error), all using only matured labels, with a 10-origin warm-up and g clipped to [0.25, 4]. In the 34 primary cells
(universes 2011-26 A, B, A+B; H = 20 and 60; all windows) no model satisfies the pre-specified criteria: ridge has lower mean QLIKE than the Gaussian correction in 27 of 34 cells (median difference -0.058; Gaussian QLIKE 0.28-0.59), but the paired interval favours it in only 2 of 34;
constant 24/34 lower and 1 favouring; tercile 23/34 and 1. Mean absolute log calibration error is lower than Gaussian in only 10 of 34 cells for ridge and in none for constant or tercile (the QLIKE-optimal multiplier pushes the median ratio away from 1);
ridge is worse than Gaussian on high-volatility origins (median +0.045), and fewer than 75% of the cells with an improvement keep it after dropping the 5% of origins with the largest gain (criterion C5). Ridge improves more often at H = 20 (94% of cells) than at H = 60 (65%).
Sensitivity (not used to select): ridge with lambda = 25 favours the second stage in 7 of 34 cells (median -0.076) and lambda = 1 in 2 of 34; adopting 25 would be a post-hoc choice and needs fresh data.
Controls on simulated paths with the development covariance: under i.i.d. Gaussian returns the second stage costs essentially nothing (|difference| <= 0.007, ridge slightly worse at W = 250: +0.007 [0.000, 0.013]);
under strong GARCH clustering it helps (ridge at W = 60: -0.065 [-0.108, -0.027]); under the regime process it does not clearly help. So the pipeline can detect volatility-state signal when it is strong, and on the Indian data it finds only weak, noise-level gains.
The development and 2011-21 sub-samples show the same pattern (mostly small negative differences with intervals covering 0). Conclusion: report c(N,T) as the calibration, with the second stage as a documented negative result, not a proposed method.

**10. The historical-sample predictor test reproduces (Table III and Section V-C).** Running the repository's pre-specified test on the re-downloaded 2011-2021 data (41 / 27 / 20 / 9 / 4 / 3 non-overlapping runs at W = 60 / 90 / 120 / 250 / 500 / 750):
the exposure scores s1, s3, s5 have pooled within-window rho 0.031, 0.102, 0.113 (Holm p = 1.00), as in the paper; log kappa and effective rank meet the full rule for both optimizers with the opposite sign to the expected one (unconstrained -0.321 and 0.297 non-overlapping, -0.357 and 0.316 stride-60);
log kappa correlates 0.785 with training-window volatility and its within-window rho falls from -0.343 to -0.218 after controlling for it (paper 0.79, -0.34 to -0.22). Gaussian signal in principle (2000 pairs, W = 60) is 0.406 / 0.560 / 0.636 (paper 0.41 / 0.56 / 0.64).
Two differences: (i) lambda_min / mean(lambda) also meets the rule for the long-only portfolio (non-overlapping rho 0.344, Holm 0.006; stride-60 0.383, Holm 0.007), which the paper's text does not mention (it names only log kappa and effective rank); the unconstrained eff. rank result is borderline (Holm 0.035);
(ii) my power analysis is a re-implementation because the paper's script is not in the repository: with window-specific effects and the observed run lengths I get power 0.60 / 0.93 / 0.99 for the ideal effect and 0.02 / 0.05 / 0.06 for the attenuated one (paper 0.48 / 0.93 / 0.99 and 0.03 / 0.06 / 0.08);
a constant effect across windows overstates power (0.89 / 1.00 / 1.00). The measured/Gaussian gap-dispersion ratio at W = 60 is 3.7 (3.7-5.7 across windows; the paper reports 3.1-4.8). The conclusions (no support for the exposure predictor, no power to detect it after attenuation, condition-type signals partly volatility) are unchanged.

**What this does not show.** Nothing here reproduces the historical-sample predictor test (Table III), regimes, or a second-stage calibrator.

Across the 58 (sample, H, W) cells (`results/journal_extension/bps_check/bps_variants.csv`) the verbatim estimator has mean QLIKE lower than my analogue by 0.025 (W=60), 0.013 (W=90), 0.008 (W=120) and <=0.002 at W>=250. Mean-difference CI below 0: 49 (analogue) vs 48 (verbatim) of 58. Conclusion: the BPS discrepancy is resolved; use the verbatim version in the journal tables.
**Novelty (literature, partial).** Dickinson (1974) shows the in-sample GMV variance is chi-square; Kan & Smith (2008, Management Science 54(7):1364-1380) derive the exact distribution of out-of-sample mean and variance of sample minimum-variance portfolios under normality; Okhrin & Schmid (2006), Kempf & Memmel (2006), Frahm & Memmel (2010), Bodnar & Schmid (2009, Eur. J. Finance) study the moments/distributions. Proposition 1 is therefore a restatement (independent derivation), NOT a new theorem; the contribution is its use as a forecast calibration, the c vs c_qlike distinction, and the dependence-aware empirical evidence. I could not read the full texts of Kan-Smith or Kempf-Memmel (PDFs blocked/binary); verify before submission.

**12. Prediction-interval coverage (Stage D, new; `results/journal_extension/intervals/interval_coverage.csv`).** Gaussian-theory central intervals for the realised block variance, h_c x quantiles of R*G/(c*Q) (exact laws; G = test-block sampling noise; simulation test shows nominal coverage under i.i.d. Gaussian data), applied to the corrected forecasts in the five conference samples (58 cells x 2 levels). Empirical coverage is far below nominal: mean 0.502 for nominal 0.80 and 0.630 for nominal 0.90; nominal inside the origin-block bootstrap interval in only 3/58 and 4/58 cells. 90% coverage falls with window length (W=60 0.745, 120 0.683, 250 0.590, 750 0.524) and is lower at H=60 (0.575) than H=20 (0.685). Misses are roughly symmetric (below 0.168, above 0.202 at 90%). Interpretation: the interval accounts for estimation and block-sampling noise only; at long windows the dominant error is that the true covariance changes between the training window and the test block, which Gaussian i.i.d. theory cannot capture. Do NOT present these intervals as usable risk-forecast intervals.
